# Reflection: custom-algo-2 and the path forward

---

## Problem statement

The **perfect-start problem**: find a move sequence for round 1 (50 ticks) that maximizes tiles captured on a generals.io-style board.

The standard opening strategy is a **burst pattern** — accumulate troops on the general, then deploy them to capture a chain of tiles. This repeats 3-6 times across 50 ticks. Key mechanics:

- General produces +1 troop every 2 ticks (first production on tick 2)
- Moving from tile A to adjacent tile B sends all-but-one troops
- Later bursts may re-traverse owned tiles (**overlap**) to reach fresh territory — costs ticks but not troops
- Feasible burst patterns (sizes, timing, overlap) can be pre-computed from the production schedule

The solver must choose both the burst pattern and the path for each burst, given board geometry (mountains, edges). The naive search space is enormous — ~300 non-backtracking paths at length 6, so 4 bursts of length 6 alone gives ~8 billion combinations. The core challenge is reducing this space efficiently.

---

## What we've learned from two solver attempts

### Algo-1: strong pruning makes brute-force work

Algo-1 pre-generates ALL non-backtracking paths from the general, pre-generates all valid timing entries, then searches for compatible pairs using grouped backtracking with multi-level pruning.

**Why it's fast on most boards:** The timing entry ordering (longest burst first, zero-overlap first) means the simplest valid solution is tried first. On most boards, the FIRST entry produces a solution — hence sub-100ms solves. Feasibility pruning (per-burst reach, aggregate reach, neighbor bottleneck) kills dead branches before candidate scanning. Grouped search amortizes candidate checks across entries with matching `(moveLen, overlap)`. Bitmask operations make all of this cache-friendly.

**Where it hits a wall:** When max captures < 24, algo-1 must prove each higher target infeasible before descending. This **descent cost** dominates runtime on hard boards — the bulk of time is spent exhaustively searching at 24, 23, 22... before finding the achievable target. Even with strong pruning, boards with difficult local geometry near the general take up to ~4s (millions of candidate checks).

### Algo-2: construction without pruning is a dead end

Algo-2 tried to exploit a structural decomposition: split the board into a near-region (handled by prefix path enumeration) and a mid-region (handled by greedy lane construction). The insight — that prefix tips are the natural interface between these sub-problems — is real.

But the prototype failed badly: orders of magnitude slower than algo-1, and often finding fewer captures. The root cause is that construction without search space reduction is just generate-and-test. Without pruning, when algo-2 can't solve at 24 captures, it descends to 23 (~50K timing entries, ~9x more) and grinds through all of them blindly. The greedy lane builder fails on nearly every attempt.

**The lane decomposition idea** — that the mid-region can be partitioned into independent non-overlapping lanes, each owned by a burst — remains a potentially useful structural concept, even though algo-2's implementation of it didn't work.

---

## The core diagnosis

**Search space reduction is the fundamental challenge.** Pruning, state-collapse, or some other way of cheaply eliminating large swaths of the search space is non-negotiable for a fast optimal solver.

This shows up in two ways:

1. **Descent cost:** When max captures < 24, the solver must prove each higher target infeasible before descending. The bulk of runtime on hard boards is spent exhaustively searching at impossible targets.

2. **Within-target grinding:** Even at the *correct* capture target, certain boards require millions of candidate checks before finding the one valid solution. Corner-9x9 is a completely open 9x9 grid with the general in the corner — 24 captures is achievable, but it takes 3.8s and 20M candidate checks. Pocket-2-11x11 is a wide-open 11x11 with a small 3-mountain pocket near the general — only takes 3 moves to exit, but still 534ms and 2M candidate checks. These boards aren't "constrained" in overall topology — the issue is local geometry near the general (corners, pockets, edges) creating overlap contention between bursts, producing huge numbers of plausible-but-failing combinations.

These are two faces of the same fundamental issue: the search space is too large to scan without stronger reduction.
