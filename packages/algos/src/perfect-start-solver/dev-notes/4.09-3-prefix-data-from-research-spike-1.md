# Prefix-Path Data from Research-Spike-1

Curated subset of findings from the research-spike-1 prefix experiments (sessions 3.23-3 through 3.23-5) that are relevant to the next-gen solver design.

This doc focuses on **individual prefix properties** — the building blocks of prefix sets, which are independent of the enumeration strategy used to combine them:

- **Prefix pool sizes** — how many candidate prefixes exist at each depth, across board types
- **Per-neighbor distribution** — how prefixes split across the general's neighbors (symmetric vs asymmetric)
- **Tip properties** — free neighbor counts at prefix tips, indicating whether suffixes can extend
- **Board-size independence** — prefix structure depends on local geometry, not board size

**What's excluded and why:** Research-spike-1 also produced set-level data (prefix set counts, dead-tip percentages), but that data was computed under either "min-depth" or "full-depth" depth strategies. Both strategies were poorly matched to the actual problem structure — min-depth gives B1 (the longest and most important burst) a depth-1 prefix that provides almost no directional constraint, while full-depth forces short bursts (B5-B6) to claim deep prefixes they don't need, exhausting the pool on constrained boards. The set-level numbers from either strategy aren't meaningful for the variable-depth-per-burst approach we're now pursuing (see the **depth dilemma** section below). We'll need new experiments for set-level analysis under variable depth.

## Prefix pool sizes (depths 1-4)

Number of non-backtracking paths from the general at each depth, across representative boards. The pool at depth D is the set of candidate prefixes available for any burst assigned depth D.

| Geometry class | Representative boards | d=1 | d=2 | d=3 | d=4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Degree-4, open | open-7x7, fairly-open, fairly-open-2 | 4 | 9-12 | 23-36 | 56-96 |
| Degree-4, semi-enclosed | medium-spacious, semi-enclosed-region, nooks-and-crannies | 4 | 7-11 | 16-28 | 29-65 |
| Degree-3, edge | edge-9x9, edge-choke-point | 3 | 5-7 | 12-19 | 27-49 |
| Degree-3, tight | big-region-tight-choke, small-corner-pocket, semi-tight-near-corner | 3 | 5-7 | 11-12 | 19-20 |
| Degree-2, open interior | corner-7x7/9x9/13x13, floating-corner, half-enclosed-half-open | 2 | 4-6 | 9-15 | 19-40 |
| Degree-2, pocketed | pocket-11x11, semi-open-with-small-pocket | 2 | 4-6 | 9-13 | 19-26 |
| Degree-2, corridor/tight | edge-pocket, edge-pocket-2, tight-edge-with-chokes | 2 | 2-4 | 2-8 | 3-14 |
| Extreme corridor | scattered-pockets-13x13 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 |

Growth factor from D=3 to D=4 is roughly 2-2.5x on open boards, less on constrained boards.

**No depth-5 data exists.** Extrapolating from growth rates: D=5 pool likely ~150-250 for open degree-4 boards, ~50-100 for degree-2/3 boards. Needs validation.

## Per-neighbor prefix distribution

Prefixes are not evenly distributed across the general's neighbors. This has direct implications for variable-depth prefix assignment.

Key patterns:
- **Symmetric boards** (corner-9x9): each neighbor gets exactly 1 -> 2 -> 5 -> 12 at depths 1-4.
- **Asymmetric boards** (fairly-open): one neighbor gets 24 prefixes at D=4 while another gets 8 — a 3x ratio. The imbalance reflects directional differences in available space.
- **Corridor neighbors**: some neighbors enter narrow corridors immediately and stay at 1-3 prefixes across all depths (e.g., on nooks-and-crannies, one neighbor gives 1 -> 1 -> 2 -> 3).
- **Dead neighbors**: tight-corner-1 has a neighbor that's a single-tile dead-end (1 -> 0 -> 0 -> 0).

**Implication for variable-depth:** The depth needed per burst depends on which neighbor it exits through. A burst through a corridor neighbor is fully determined at D=2. A burst through an open neighbor needs D=4+ for meaningful directional commitment. This validates the variable-depth approach.

## Tip properties (free neighbors at prefix tips)

The "free" count at a tip = number of walkable neighbors not occupied by the prefix itself (measured per individual prefix, not against a prefix set). This indicates whether the suffix can extend in multiple directions (free >= 2) or is forced into a corridor (free = 1) or dead (free = 0).

**Open / semi-open boards at D=4:**
- free=0: rare (~0-3% of tips)
- free=1: uncommon (~5-17%)
- free=2: common (~20-55%)
- free=3: common (~25-72%)

Most tips have escaped the constrained near-general zone by D=4.

**Constrained boards at D=4:**
- free=1 is much more common: 20-50% of tips still in corridor-like zones
- Examples: nooks-and-crannies (38% free=1), edge-1 (42% free=1), big-region-tight-choke (~47% free=1)
- free=0 remains rare (only with dead-end neighbors)

**Depth progression (corner-9x9 reference):**

| Depth | free=0 | free=1 | free=2 | free=3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | 0 | 2 | 0 |
| 2 | 0 | 0 | 2 | 2 |
| 3 | 0 | 2 | 2 | 6 |
| 4 | 0 | 2 | 8 | 14 |

**Implication for near/mid handoff:** On open boards, D=3-4 is sufficient for tips to reach open space where lane decomposition can take over. On constrained boards, some tips are still in forced corridors at D=4 — for these, the "lane" beyond the tip is a single-path corridor (which the corridor model handles directly and trivially).

## Board-size independence

Corner-7x7, corner-9x9, and corner-13x13 all have nearly identical prefix data (2 -> 4 -> 10 -> 24 at depths 1-4) and identical tip properties. Prefix structure is determined by local geometry around the general, not board size.

This validates a core assumption: prefix enumeration cost depends on local topology only. Board size affects the mid-region (lane lengths, available space) but not the near-region.

## The depth dilemma (context for variable-depth design)

Research-spike-1 tried two extremes for how deep each burst's prefix should be during prefix set enumeration:

- **Min-depth**: `depth = max(overlap + 1, 1)` per burst. The minimum needed to express the overlap constraint. Zero-overlap bursts (including B1, which is always zero-overlap) get depth 1 — just which neighbor of the general they exit through. This keeps set counts manageable, but B1's "prefix" is essentially meaningless: a depth-1 prefix provides no directional commitment for a burst that extends 8-12 tiles. The whole point of prefixes is to capture directional structure, and min-depth fails to do that for the longest burst.

- **Full-depth**: all bursts forced to D=4 regardless of their length. This gives every burst real directional commitment, but forces short bursts (B5-B6, path length 1-4) to claim D=4 prefixes they don't need. On constrained boards with a prefix pool of 2-4, there simply aren't enough distinct D=4 paths to assign one to each of 5-6 bursts — set counts drop to zero. On open boards, set counts explode (87-345x increase) because B1 at D=4 has ~24-60 unconstrained choices.

Neither extreme is well-suited to the problem. The natural solution is variable depth per burst — deeper prefixes for long bursts (B1-B2 at D=4-5), shallower for short bursts (B5-B6 at D=1-2 or just their full path length). This was identified as a direction but never tested in research-spike-1. See the brainstorm doc (4.09-1) for the current design direction.
