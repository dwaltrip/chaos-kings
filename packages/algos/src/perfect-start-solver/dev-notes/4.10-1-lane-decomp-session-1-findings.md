# Session 1: Lane Decomposition — Initial Exhaustive Enumeration Findings

First pass of experiments with the `custom-algo-2/lane-decomp` prototype. We built an exhaustive decomposer (enumerate all non-backtracking paths per requested length from every frontier tile, then nested-loop for non-overlapping k-tuples) and ran it across board types, lane counts, and blob seeds to understand how constrained the problem actually is.

Related docs:
- [Lane decomposition brainstorm](4.09-4-lane-decomposition-brainstorm.md) — approach catalog, the "good enough" principle, capacity framing.
- [Formalizing solver ideas](4.09-1-brainstorm-for-formalizing-solver-ideas.md) — overall framework, §4 is the mid-region focus.
- [Burst segmentation analysis](4.09-2-burst-segmentation-analysis.md) — which bursts extend into the mid-region.

## Setup

- **Tool:** `custom-algo-2/lane-decomp/run-lane-decomp.ts`
- **Approach:** exhaustive enumeration — for each requested lane length, enumerate all non-backtracking paths of that length from every frontier tile; then nested loop to find all valid k-tuples of mutually non-overlapping lanes.
- **Blobs:** union of 3-6 descending-length non-backtracking random walks from a shared start. Seeded via mulberry32.
- **What we varied:** board, blob seed, lane count `k`, lane lengths.
- **What we held fixed for most runs:** start tile = board's general, blob overlap budget = 0.

CLI invocation pattern:

```
tools/run-from-algos.sh packages/algos/src/perfect-start-solver/custom-algo-2/lane-decomp/run-lane-decomp.ts \
  --board <name> [--start x,y] \
  --blob-paths N --blob-max-len N --blob-min-len N --blob-overlap N \
  --seed N \
  --lanes L1,L2,... \
  --max-decomps N
```

## Performance

The search itself is fast: <2 s for 10 M decompositions across the runs below. The ceiling we hit was **memory**, not compute — accumulating millions of lane-tuple objects triggers V8 OOM before the search bogs down. Implications for future sessions:

- No algorithmic optimization was needed for session 1. Feasibility of the problem as-posed is a clear "yes, tractable."
- If we want true uncapped counts on spacious boards, we need a count-only mode that increments a counter instead of allocating `Lane[]` arrays. Noted as future work.

## Scaling by k (lane count)

Fixed params: `--blob-paths 4 --blob-max-len 4 --blob-min-len 2 --blob-overlap 0 --seed 1`. Lane length lists use a descending pattern that roughly matches typical burst segmentation.

| Board | k=2 `[5,3]` | k=3 `[6,4,3]` | k=4 `[5,4,3,2]` | k=5 `[6,5,4,3,2]` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| open-11x11 | 42 746 | >10 M (capped) | >10 M (capped, 1.7 s) | OOM at 50 M cap; >1 M @ 1 M cap |
| corner-9x9 ⁴ | 4 079 | 212 728 | 301 624 | 4 874 946 |

⁴ `--start 1,1` — a hand-picked corner start, not the board's general (this board file doesn't have a meaningful general position for our purposes).

**Takeaway:** super-exponential on open boards, ~1000× growth from k=2 to k=5 on corner-9x9. Shape of the curve varies dramatically by board geometry. At k=4 on corner-9x9 the count dips relative to k=3 because the lengths were shifted down — k is not independent of length.

**Seed variation takeaways:**
- On saturated blobs (maze-7x7), seed impact is zero — mulberry32 + 4-way branching + short walks converge to the same shape.

## General position matters — a lot

Two realistic 25×25 boards tested first with an arbitrary "board center" start, then with the actual baked-in general position. The results inverted.

### tight-corner-1

General at `(24, 20)` — literally inside a tight pocket near the SE corner.

| Start | Params | k=3 Result |
|---|---|---|
| board center (arbitrary) | `--blob-paths 6 --blob-max-len 5 --blob-min-len 3 --lanes 8,6,4 --max-decomps 1000000` | capped at 1 M every seed |
| general (24,20) | same | **6 of 8 seeds have 0 decompositions**; seeds 5 and 7 → 4484 each |

Visualization from seed 1: 62 L8 candidates all funnel through a 2-3 tile wide chute near `(22, 17-21)`. Any pair of L8 paths must share tiles in that chute.

### semi-enclosed-region

General at `(16, 2)` — top edge, actually pretty open from the general's perspective.

| Start | Params | Result |
|---|---|---|
| board center (arbitrary) | `--blob-paths 6 --blob-max-len 5 --blob-min-len 3 --lanes 8,6,4 --max-decomps 1000000` | 0 to 18 797 decomps across 8 seeds; seed 6 = failure |
| general (16,2) | same | capped at 1 M every seed |

The "failures" we saw starting from board center were **artifacts of a bad start position**, not the board. From the actual general (on an edge), plenty of space exists.

**Takeaway:** the general's position dominates. Our small-board runs had used hand-picked corners or centers. The CLI has since been updated to default `--start` to the board's general tile. Any future analysis should use the real general unless explicitly probing an alternate scenario.

## The capacity question

We initially hypothesized a clean **topological capacity** (max-flow / min-cut sense): one number per `(board, blob)` that bounds the achievable `k` regardless of lane length. On tight-corner-1 we briefly thought we'd found one — `k=2 [8,6]` works, `k=3 [8,6,4]` mostly fails, `k=4 [8,6,4,3]` always fails — and called that "capacity = 2."

To verify, we varied lane *length* at fixed `k` on tight-corner-1:

| Seed | `[2,2,2]` | `[3,3,3]` | `[4,4,4]` | `[8,6,4]` | `[2,2,2,2]` | `[3,3,3,3]` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 12 ✓ | 0 ✗ | 0 ✗ | 0 ✗ | 0 ✗ | 0 ✗ |
| 5 | 24 ✓ | 174 ✓ | 252 ✓ | 4484 ✓ | 24 ✓ | 144 ✓ |

Params: `--blob-paths 6 --blob-max-len 5 --blob-min-len 3 --blob-overlap 0 --max-decomps 1000000` (general start).

Two seeds, same board and general, produce **completely different empirical capacities**. Seed 1 caps at `k=3 [2,2,2]`; seed 5 handles `k=4 [8,6,4,3]` mostly fine, only failing at the higher lengths. Length matters.

**Conclusions:**

1. **Empirical "capacity" is a joint function of `(blob shape, k, lane lengths)` — not just the board.** It's not a single number. The quantity we actually observe is a cliff: for each `(board, blob, k)` there's a maximum sustainable lane length above which decomposition fails.

2. **Topological (max-flow) capacity is an upper bound, but not a tight filter.** A `max-flow = 3` result doesn't guarantee that `k=3` succeeds at the requested lengths — three long lanes can still collide inside a chute even if three short ones wouldn't.

3. **Useful feasibility filters need to account for lane length**, bringing them closer to "face projection with depth measurement" than to pure max-flow. This suggests face-projection is worth prototyping early since it naturally encodes "depth available in a direction."

4. **Different blob shapes expose different effective capacity** even on the same board and general. Frontier-tile location — not just count — shapes the reachable path space.

## Open-vs-constrained summary

| Regime | Behavior | Solver implication |
|---|---|---|
| Open (open-11x11, semi-enclosed general pos) | Thousands to millions of decompositions | Any heuristic works; decomposition is a trivial step |
| Mixed (corner-9x9, dense-mtns-9x9) | Hundreds to thousands, occasional shape-dependent dips | Heuristics work on average; pathological blobs exist but are rare |
| Tight (tight-corner-1, maze-7x7 with long lanes) | 0 to a few dozen; length cliffs; blob-shape-sensitive | Algorithmic choice matters here; this is the regime that makes or breaks the solver |

The "interesting" regime for future session work is the tight end of this spectrum — that's where decomposition is real work and where a smart algorithm pays off.

## Pointers for future sessions

1. **Count-only mode.** Skip the decomp-object accumulation, just count. Unlocks true totals on spacious boards and removes the OOM ceiling.
2. **Canonical shape counting.** Many of the reported decompositions are near-duplicates (same lane 1, varying lane 2 tails). A "distinct topological shapes" count would be much smaller and more meaningful than raw decomposition counts.
3. **Blob generator determinism.** Fix RNG collisions (seen on corner-9x9) so that seed variation produces genuinely different blobs. Probably needs a richer RNG or more deliberate diversity in walk selection.
4. **Max-flow / face-projection prototype.** Fast feasibility filter. Worth prototyping to see how well it tracks actual success/failure, and whether length-aware face projection beats pure max-flow at predicting the length cliff.
5. **Length-cliff characterization.** For a fixed `(board, blob)`, map the boundary function `k → max sustainable lane length`. Might reveal clean structure (e.g., approximately piecewise linear) that would let us replace exhaustive enumeration with a closed-form feasibility check for many cases.
6. **Solver-realistic blobs.** Our blobs come from a toy union-of-random-walks generator. Eventually we want to feed in actual prefix sets from the solver's prefix enumerator so failures/successes reflect real operating conditions.
