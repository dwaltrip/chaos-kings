# Session 3.23-3 — Ideas and Next Directions

Brainstorming from the prefix enumeration results. What to explore next, what questions are open, and how the pieces connect.

## What the experiments confirmed

The prefix structure theory's core predictions held up:
- Prefix pools are small (tens, not thousands)
- Fan-out narrowing is steep (depth-4 prefix gives ~20-30x reduction from full path pool)
- Most prefixes "open up" (free≥2 at the tip)
- The solution's bursts use prefixes from the enumerated pool, and overlap bursts share early prefix tiles as predicted

The theory is directionally right. The question is no longer "does prefix structure exist?" but "how do we exploit it?"

## Next analyses

### Characterizing the prefix-suffix boundary

The prefix experiments measured the prefix *pool* (how many, how they branch). The next question is: what does the space look like where prefixes end and suffixes begin?

Two angles on this, one board-level and one per-prefix:

- **Suffix zone capacity.** BFS distance histogram from the general. For each prefix depth D, suffix zone = walkable tiles at distance > D. This is a board-level capacity check: is there physically enough room for multiple suffixes? If the suffix zone has 200 tiles and two bursts each need ~8, there's obviously room. If it's 25, they're competing. Cheap to compute (one BFS per board). Especially relevant for trajectory independence — when 2-3 long bursts need to extend beyond their prefixes, this tells you whether the space exists at all.

- **Per-prefix expansion profiles.** BFS from each prefix's tip, excluding tiles in the prefix path. Count reachable tiles at distance 1, 2, 3... from the tip, aggregate as distribution stats across all prefixes at a given depth. This is the finer question: free=2 at the tip could mean the space opens to 10 tiles at distance 2, or it could mean both branches hit walls immediately. Relationship to fan-out: fan-out counts paths (combinatorial), expansion counts tiles (geometric). High expansion + high fan-out = genuinely open space. High expansion + low fan-out = room but winding corridors.

There may be other useful characterizations too. The general framing is: what can we learn about prefix tips and the frontier beyond them? The two analyses above are specific questions within that, but we should stay open to other angles as they come up.

### Prefix set enumeration

The central next experiment. For a given burst configuration (burst count + overlap assignments), how many mutually compatible prefix sets exist? The theory conjectures tens to low hundreds. This is where the theory either validates or doesn't.

**Overlap must be integrated, not separate.** Real solutions mix overlap and non-overlap bursts. corner-9x9's solution has overlaps [0, 3, 2, 1, 0] — four bursts through the same neighbor, diverging via overlap at different depths. Enumerating only zero-overlap (all-disjoint) sets would miss this entirely.

Overlap changes the compatibility constraints fundamentally:
- Zero-overlap bursts: prefixes must be tile-disjoint
- Overlap-K bursts: prefix's first K tiles must be IN the covered set (union of prior bursts)

These aren't the same constraint with different parameters. A burst with overlap=3 actively *requires* shared tiles with prior coverage, rather than avoiding them. Zero-overlap falls out as the simplest case of a single analysis parameterized by overlap config.

**Sequential construction.** Overlap creates ordering dependencies. B2's prefix choices depend on B1's coverage. B3's depend on B1+B2. Prefix sets are built incrementally, burst by burst — you can't independently choose all prefixes and then check compatibility. This is a form of shallow backtracking over tiny domains (the theory doc calls this out).

**Where the overlap config comes from.** The timing entries already enumerate which bursts overlap and by how much. Each timing entry implies an overlap configuration. So prefix set enumeration is naturally tied to timing entries — for each entry's overlap config, enumerate compatible prefix sets.

**What we'd learn.** Whether the conjecture holds (tens to low hundreds of sets). How the count varies across boards and across overlap configs. Whether constrained boards have fewer sets or just different ones. Whether the solver's solution always appears in the enumerated pool. And concretely: how many full-length candidates remain per burst within a prefix set, compared to the current solver's candidate counts.

### Summary of open analyses

1. **Characterizing the prefix-suffix boundary** — what does the space look like where prefixes end and suffixes begin?
   - **Suffix zone capacity** (board-level) — "Is there room beyond the prefixes?"
   - **Per-prefix expansion** (per-prefix) — "Does each prefix lead to room?"
2. **Prefix set enumeration** — "How many compatible prefix assignments exist?"
   - Parameterized by overlap config (from timing entries). Zero-overlap = all-disjoint is the simplest case.
   - Sequential construction: B2's choices depend on B1's coverage, B3 on B1+B2, etc.
   - The theory's central conjecture is tens to low hundreds of sets. Indirect evidence is encouraging: pools are small (24 prefixes at depth 4 on corner boards), zero-overlap pairs are bounded by per-neighbor counts (12 × 12 = 144 on degree-2), and overlap constraints tighten further. Hard to get thousands of viable sets from 24 candidates.
   - Key sub-questions: how do counts vary across boards and overlap configs? Does the solver's solution always appear in the pool?
3. **Candidate scoping** (prefix set → solver) — "How many full-length candidates per burst within a prefix set?"

## Open questions

- What prefix depth is "enough"? Depth 3 captures most of the narrowing; depth 4 adds some. Does the extra depth matter more for compatibility than for fan-out reduction?
- Dead prefixes (zero fan-out at a target length) — could we prune these cheaply up front, rather than having the solver discover them the hard way?
