### Group ordering experiment (negative result)

- Implemented fewest-candidates-first ordering (replace longest-first sort in `buildTimingGroups`)
- Result: 3-8x regression on most hard boards
  - floating-corner-11x11: 825ms → 6,700ms (8x slower)
  - tight-corner-2: 412ms → 1,320ms (3x slower)
  - corner-7x7: entries dropped 10x (36K → 3.6K) but wall time slightly worse
- Root cause: short burst-1 groups have fewer candidates but trigger deeper recursion (more remaining bursts). Each candidate is more expensive to evaluate.
- **Conclusion:** Longest-first ordering is already good. The real waste on tight-corner boards is at the capture-target level (exhausting captures=24 and 23 before finding solution at 22), not group ordering.
- Reverted to original ordering.

## Key learnings

- **Realistic boards are not a scaling challenge** — all 6 solve in <500ms. The solver's performance on toy boards was misleading in terms of what needs optimizing.
- **Path generation is a significant fixed cost** — 20-60ms on 25x25 boards, dominates on easy boards.
- **Capture-target iteration may be a source of waste** — tight-corner boards get 22 captures, meaning the solver exhausts all timing groups at captures=24 and 23 before finding anything. Per-target timing hasn't been measured yet.
- **Longest-first group ordering is already good** — trying to optimize it made things worse.
- **Symmetry breaking has narrow applicability** — only helps on symmetric degree-2 boards, which are rare in practice.
