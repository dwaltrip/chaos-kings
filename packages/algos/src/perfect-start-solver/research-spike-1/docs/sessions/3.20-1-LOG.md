### Experiment results

**Main finding: redundancy is minimal.** Compression ratios are 1.1–1.4x across all boards, all lengths. Zero dominated masks anywhere. The survey hypothesized 10–50x on open boards — the actual number is ~1.2x. This is a definitive negative result for dedup (4.1).

**Why**: Non-backtracking paths on a grid are nearly 1:1 with their masks. Different tile orderings usually require different spatial routes that visit different tiles. The contiguity constraint kills the combinatorial explosion we expected.

**What this rules out**: Path dedup, inverted-index search, cheap arc consistency via domain reduction.

### Follow-up: starting-neighbor distribution

Quick follow-up measuring how paths split across the general's neighbors. Symmetric boards split perfectly evenly. Asymmetric boards show 2–4x imbalance between strongest and weakest neighbor at longer path lengths. This suggests direction-based candidate partitioning could be a cheap win — partition paths by `tiles[0]` and only scan the relevant partition per burst. Noted as a tentative insight, not yet validated.
