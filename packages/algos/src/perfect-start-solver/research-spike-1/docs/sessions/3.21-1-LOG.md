### L3 pruning

- Extracted `bfsCumulativeMasks` into `board-bfs.ts` — shared BFS utility with 5 tests
- Replaced both inline BFS implementations in solver-v3 with calls to shared helper
- Added `blankMasks` field to `NeighborInfo` (per-neighbor reachable tiles by distance)
- Implemented L3 check in partition loop: for zero-overlap bursts, verify neighbor territory has enough blank tiles for required captures
- **Result: zero additional prunes on all 29 test boards**
- Analysis: per-neighbor spatial feasibility isn't the binding constraint. On degree-2 corners (all hard boards), the surviving neighbor has access to ~half the board — per-neighbor check adds nothing over global feasibility. See `findings/3.21-l3-per-neighbor-pruning.md`.
- Decision: keep in solver — cheap insurance, and the implementation produced useful infrastructure

### End-of-session review

Opus sub-agent reviewed L3 implementation and then stepped back to assess the broader research spike. Key feedback:

**L3 assessment**: Correct implementation. Zero-prune result is a strong signal that spatial feasibility isn't the binding constraint — bottleneck is combinatorial (too many compatible candidates), not spatial (not enough tiles). This should inform the pivot to next approaches.

**Recommended next directions (in priority order)**:
1. **Symmetry breaking** — one `continue` statement, instant 2x on symmetric degree-2 boards. Overdue.
2. **Group ordering** — solver exhausts all longer-burst-1 groups before shorter ones. ~10 lines, significant practical win.
3. **CSP / constraint propagation** — most promising structural idea, heaviest to implement. Forced-move propagation is the entry point.

**Gaps identified**:
- Test suite may not be hard enough — worst case is ~1.5s. Symmetry + group ordering could bring everything under 200ms. Need to clarify: optimize current boards, or handle intractable ones?
- Need test boards with **asymmetric neighbor territories** (e.g., general at the mouth of a corridor) to exercise L3 and future spatial ideas
- Phase timing (search vs path-gen breakdown) was never measured — assumption that search dominates is untested
- Overlap bursts get much less optimization attention than zero-overlap
