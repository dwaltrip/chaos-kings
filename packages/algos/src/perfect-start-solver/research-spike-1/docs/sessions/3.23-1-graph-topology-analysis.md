# Session notes: Graph Topology Analysis (Thread 7)

**Date**: 2026-03-23
**Session**: 3.23-1

## What was done

Built graph topology infrastructure (`utils/board-graph.ts`) and ran two experiments across all 46 boards:

1. **Global topology** (`experiments/3.23-1-graph-topology.ts`) — articulation points, corridors, region decomposition, solve times.
2. **Local structure within burst range** (`experiments/3.23-2-local-structure.ts`) — classifies every tile within distance 12 of the general as pocket, corridor, or open.

### Definitions

- **Corridor tile**: walkable tile with exactly 2 walkable neighbors. Forced-path — any burst through this tile has no choice of direction.
- **Pocket**: a gated region (far side of a cut vertex relative to the general) + the cut vertex itself + any degree-2 corridor tiles chaining from the cut vertex back toward the general. The corridor opening is part of the pocket because it's a forced path into a dead end.
- **Corridor** (in the local structure analysis): degree-2 tile that is NOT part of a pocket. A forced-path segment between two open areas — constrained but not dead-end.
- **Open**: everything else.

When a tile is both a corridor tile and a pocket tile, pocket wins (stronger constraint).

## Infrastructure built

`utils/board-graph.ts` — reusable module, sibling to `custom-algo-1/`. Exports:
- `getWalkableNeighbors`, `getWalkableDegree`, `getWalkableTiles` — adjacency helpers
- `findCorridors` — degree-2 tiles + assembled chain segments with endpoints
- `tarjan` — articulation points + bridges, single DFS pass
- `decomposeRegions` — flood-fill after removing cut vertices, returns regions with adjacent cut vertex sets

All take `FlatBoard`, return clean data structures. Tests in `utils/__tests__/board-graph.test.ts`.

### Observations

**Slow simple boards have zero articulation points.** Every slow board (>300ms) in the simple set — corner-7x7, corner-9x9, corner-13x13, pocket-11x11, floating-corner-11x11, edge-pocket-*, pocket-2-11x11 — has 0 articulation points and 1 region. Topologically wide open. Exception: scattered-pockets-13x13 (2792ms) has 35 cut vertices, but it's a fragmented board, not a topology-free one.

**Realistic boards all have dozens of cut vertices (28-94).** Region decomposition shows one dominant region (275-670 tiles) in every case, but numerous boards also have non-trivial secondary regions of 20-100 tiles (e.g. fairly-open has 90 and 30; big-region-with-tight-choke has 49, 14, 14; half-enclosed-half-open has 36, 13, 9, 8, 7; small-corner-pocket has 52, 9, 7, 7). Many cut vertices gate only tiny 1-3 tile fragments, but the mid-sized pockets are common and potentially meaningful.

**tight-edge-with-chokes** is the most dramatic: three substantial regions (189, 146, 108) — genuine structural decomposition into large sections.

**Degree is the strongest correlate of difficulty.** Across both simple and realistic boards, degree-2 (and degree-1) generals dominate the slow boards. Degree-3+ boards are generally fast regardless of topology.

## Key observations

### Two distinct populations of slow boards

**Population 1: Structurally constrained.** High pocket/corridor percentage near the general. The terrain forces paths through narrow passages and dead ends.
- tight-corner-1 (564ms): 95% pocket
- tight-corner-2 (1372ms): 100% pocket
- scattered-pockets-13x13 (2792ms): 88% pocket

**Population 2: Structurally open.** Zero or near-zero pocket tiles, very low corridor. Wide-open topology — the board doesn't constrain paths at all.
- corner-7x7 (1084ms): 0% pocket, 6% constrained
- corner-9x9 (3941ms): 0% pocket, 3% constrained
- corner-13x13 (4640ms): 0% pocket, 2% constrained
- pocket-11x11 (2149ms): 0% pocket, 4% constrained
- floating-corner-11x11 (1651ms): 0% pocket, 18% constrained
- edge-pocket-2-9x9 (1080ms): 0% pocket, 16% constrained

These two populations likely need different optimization strategies.

### Pockets near the general constrain movement heavily on realistic boards

tight-corner-1 has a 6-tile pocket gated by a cut vertex at distance 1 from the general, plus corridor tiles threading between mountains. The general is boxed in with only 2 open tiles in its entire 12-distance burst range.

tight-corner-2 has the entire board behind a single chokepoint at distance 1 (degree-1 general). Every tile within burst range is pocket.

### Topology correlates with speed on structured boards but not on open ones

For degree-3+ boards, higher structural constraint (more pockets/corridors) correlates with *faster* solve times — the structure constrains paths, reducing the search space. maze-9x9 (75% constrained) solves in 4.7ms. open-7x7 (8% constrained) takes 85ms.

For degree-2 open boards, topology gives zero signal. corner-9x9 and corner-13x13 are topologically identical (0% constrained) but take 3.9s and 4.6s respectively.

### The "expansion profile" idea (open-ended)

Discussion surfaced a loose idea: per-tile "expansion potential" — how fast does the reachable frontier grow when you BFS outward from a tile? Open-center tiles expand fast (quadratic growth). Corner tiles expand slower. Corridor tiles expand linearly. Pocket tiles flatline quickly.

The intuition is that this could predict path diversity from board structure — a tile with fast expansion has many non-overlapping paths through it, while a tile where expansion flatlines means paths compete for the same tiles. However, this is more of an open-ended framing / thought experiment at this stage. It would require more brainstorming and possibly an "aha" jump or two to connect it to something concretely useful for the solver.
