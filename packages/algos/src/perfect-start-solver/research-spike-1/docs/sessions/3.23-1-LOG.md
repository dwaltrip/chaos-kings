# Session 3.23-1 — Log

## What happened

**Thread 7: Graph topology analysis — infrastructure + two experiments.**

### Infrastructure

Built `utils/board-graph.ts` — reusable graph topology module (sibling to `custom-algo-1/`). Exports adjacency helpers, corridor detection (degree-2 chains), Tarjan's algorithm (articulation points + bridges), and region decomposition. Tests in `utils/__tests__/board-graph.test.ts`.

### Experiment 1: Global topology

Ran articulation points, corridors, and region decomposition across all 46 boards.

Key findings:
- Slow simple boards (corner-*, pocket-*, edge-pocket-*) have **zero articulation points** — topologically wide open.
- Realistic boards have dozens of cut vertices (28-94). All have one dominant region, but numerous boards also have non-trivial secondary regions of 20-100 tiles.
- Degree is the strongest correlate of difficulty — degree-2 and degree-1 generals dominate the slow boards regardless of topology.

### Experiment 2: Local structure within burst range

Classified every tile within distance 12 of the general as pocket, corridor, or open. Pocket = gated region + cut vertex + corridor opening (degree-2 chain toward general).

Revealed **two distinct populations of slow boards**:
- **Structurally constrained**: tight-corner-1 (95% pocket), tight-corner-2 (100% pocket), scattered-pockets-13x13 (88% pocket). Terrain forces paths through narrow passages and dead ends.
- **Structurally open**: corner-9x9 (3% constrained), corner-13x13 (2%), pocket-11x11 (4%). Wide-open topology — difficulty is purely combinatorial from degree-2 search space splitting.

### Discussion

- Small pockets near the general matter more than the global topology suggests — they constrain the first few steps of bursts even if they're only a few tiles.
- "Structural features within burst range" emerged as the right lens for connecting topology to solver performance.
- Discussed an "expansion profile" idea (per-tile frontier growth rate) as an open-ended thought experiment — would need more brainstorming to make concretely useful.
- The two populations likely need different optimization strategies.
