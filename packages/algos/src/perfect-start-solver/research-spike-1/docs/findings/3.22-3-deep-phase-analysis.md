# Finding: Deep Phase Analysis (Threads 1 + 10, Enhanced)

**Date**: 2026-03-22
**Session**: 3.22-3
**Verdict**: Three distinct performance regimes identified. Board geometry near the general — not board size — is the primary driver. Timing entry explosion is the key mechanism behind infeasible-target waste. Clear optimization targets for threads 5 and 10.

## Summary

Enhanced the per-target profiling (searchCalls, totalTimingEntries, feasibility breakdown per target) and ran across 26 boards (11 slow small, 6 realistic 25x25, 9 new 30x30) with 3-run medians. This answers the three open questions from session 3.22-2 and reveals the full performance landscape.

## Three performance regimes

| Regime | Driver | Boards | Lever |
|--------|--------|--------|-------|
| **Infeasible-target waste** | Timing entry explosion at lower capture targets | tight-corner-1/2, edge-pocket-2, pocket-11x11, scattered-pockets-13x13, tight-edge-with-chokes (30x30) | Thread 5 (capture-target pre-check) |
| **Deep recursive search** | Candidate scanning waste (~90%) across many search calls | corner-7x7/9x9/13x13, floating-corner-11x11, half-enclosed-half-open, semi-open-with-small-pocket (30x30) | Thread 10 (watched literals) |
| **Mixed** | Both infeasible-target waste AND deep search at the solving target | edge-pocket-9x9 (68% infeasible waste + 346ms search at cap=23) | Thread 5 + 10 |
| **Path generation** | 40-280ms path gen dominates trivial search | open-9x9/11x11/13x13, medium-spacious, fairly-open (30x30) | Thread 3/13 |

## Q1: Why do infeasible targets cost so much with zero candidate scans?

**Answer: Timing entries per group explode combinatorially as capture target decreases.**

| Capture target | Total timing entries | Entries/group | Growth vs cap=24 |
|----------------|---------------------|---------------|-------------------|
| 24 | 5,425 | 678 | 1x |
| 23 | 34,724 | 3,858 | **5.7x** |
| 22 | 75,609 | 8,401 | **12.4x** |
| 21 | 96,613 | 10,735 | 15.8x |
| 20 | 98,420 | 10,936 | 16.1x |

Each burst-1 candidate calls `searchGrouped`, which feasibility-checks every entry in the group. At cap=23, that's 3,858 entries per call — each requiring BigInt popcount on 625-bit masks. The time scales linearly with entries:

**tight-corner-2:**
- cap=24: 4,562 B1 calls × 678 entries/group = 230μs/call → **1050ms**
- cap=23: 4,572 B1 calls × 3,858 entries/group = 1,248μs/call → **5708ms** (5.4x ratio matches 5.7x entry ratio)

All feasibility-checked entries are killed (0% pass rate at infeasible targets). The feasibility checks are doing useful work — correctly pruning — but the pruning is applied redundantly to thousands of entries that all share the same infeasibility.

**Key structural point:** Timing entry counts are board-independent — they depend only on solver config (maxTicks, maxBurst, maxBursts, maxOverlap). Every board sees the same 5,425 entries at cap=24, the same 34,724 at cap=23, etc. The board determines which targets are *feasible*, not the cost of checking each one.

**Extreme cases:**
- **scattered-pockets-13x13** — solves at cap=20, wasting time on 4 infeasible targets (cap=24 through 21). At cap=21: 10,735 entries/group, 6,241μs per B1 call. 98.4% of total time wasted.
- **pocket-11x11** — solves at cap=23 in 0.3ms, but spends 3,365ms (99.2% of time) on infeasible cap=24 alone. Thread 5 would turn this from 3.4s to ~26ms.

## Q2: What drives half-enclosed-half-open's 667ms with only 7,244 candidate checks?

**Answer: 18,748 searchGrouped calls, each feasibility-checking ~22 entries, with 99.8% killed by neighbor check.**

- 17,933 burst-1 candidates, each calling searchGrouped once
- Per call: ~22 entries checked for neighbor feasibility
- 99.8% killed by N check (after burst-1 covers neighbors, subsequent zero-overlap bursts can't find blank neighbors)
- Only 0.4 candidates checked per search call on average
- Cost is feasibility checking × entries, not candidate scanning

This is the same mechanism as tight-corner boards but at a single target (cap=24) — the board has constrained neighbor access, so most timing entries' burst-2+ can't proceed.

## Q3: What does 90% scan waste on corner boards look like?

**Answer: Deep recursive search with many calls, each scanning ~9 candidates and passing ~1.**

| Board | Search calls | Cands/call | us/call | Waste |
|-------|-------------|------------|---------|-------|
| corner-7x7 | 471,881 | 9.4 | 3.8 | 90.2% |
| corner-9x9 | 776,034 | 9.0 | 7.4 | 89.6% |
| corner-13x13 | 815,176 | 8.9 | 7.6 | 89.5% |
| floating-corner-11x11 | 465,158 | 11.6 | 5.8 | 91.6% |

The waste is spread uniformly across hundreds of thousands of recursive calls. Each call is cheap (4-8μs) but there are so many. Feasibility kills 86-89% of entries (mostly by N check), leaving ~5% that pass to candidate scanning. The 90% candidate waste is within that surviving 5%.

This is the regime where thread 10 (watched literals / event-driven invalidation) would help — instead of scanning all candidates at each node, propagate invalidation only through affected candidates.

## Board size is not the driver

The 30x30 board suite (9 boards) confirms this decisively:

| Board (30x30) | Median | Regime |
|----------------|--------|--------|
| nooks-and-crannies | 8ms | Fast |
| big-region-with-tight-choke | 10ms | Fast |
| semi-tight-near-corner | 13ms | Fast |
| small-corner-pocket | 19ms | Light search |
| edge-1 | 44ms | Mixed |
| fairly-open | 55ms | Path gen |
| fairly-open-2 | 54ms | Path gen |
| **tight-edge-with-chokes** | **1408ms** | **Infeasible target** |
| **semi-open-with-small-pocket** | **2083ms** | **Deep search** |

7 of 9 boards at 30x30 solve in under 55ms. The two slow boards have the same structural properties as slow smaller boards: constrained general position creating either infeasible-target waste or deep search.

For comparison: corner-9x9 (81 tiles) takes 5.8 seconds while fairly-open-30x30 (900 tiles) takes 55ms. Board geometry near the general matters far more than board size.

## Feasibility check effectiveness

Across all boards, the neighbor check (N) is the dominant feasibility filter:

| Regime | N kill % | P kill % | A kill % | Pass % |
|--------|----------|----------|----------|--------|
| Corner boards (deep search) | 86-89% | 1% | 6-7% | 4-5% |
| Tight-corner (infeasible) | 74-96% | 0-2% | 3-25% | 0% |
| Half-enclosed-like | 98-99% | 0% | 0% | 0-1% |
| Easy boards | 24-57% | 0% | 0% | 43-76% |

The perBurst (P) check almost never fires (<2%). The aggregate (A) check fires meaningfully on tight-corner boards (up to 25% at certain targets). On infeasible targets, Pass is 0% — every single entry is correctly killed, but only after being individually checked.

## Implications for next steps

### Thread 5 (capture-target pre-check) — highest leverage for infeasible-target regime

The data shows: at infeasible targets, 100% of entries fail feasibility at every burst-1 call. A target-level pre-check could skip the entire target — avoiding both the timing table build (5-42ms) and all search calls. On tight-corner-2, this eliminates 6.8 seconds of waste.

The cheapest approach: count total reachable blank tiles from the general; if less than target captures, skip. On tight-corner boards with ~22 reachable tiles, cap=24 and cap=23 are immediately impossible.

### Thread 10 (watched literals) — highest leverage for deep-search regime

90% candidate waste across 471K-815K search calls on corner boards. Event-driven invalidation would avoid scanning candidates already known to be dead. The 9-candidates-per-call metric suggests small per-call gains but across hundreds of thousands of calls.

### Thread 3/13 (path gen) — needed for sub-50ms on open boards

Path gen takes 40-280ms on open boards. For the sub-50ms stretch goal, this is the binding constraint on easy boards.
