# Finding: 3.2 Neighbor Partitioning (Level 1)

**Date**: 2026-03-20
**Session**: 3.20-2
**Verdict**: Meaningful speedup on hard boards (1.28–1.41x). No benefit on easy boards. Confirms candidate scanning is a real bottleneck on corner-general boards.

## Summary

We partitioned path candidates by their starting neighbor (`tiles[0]`) and modified the search loop to skip irrelevant partitions based on `coveredMask`. This is the simplest version (Level 1) of the pipeline idea from STATUS — same search structure, just fewer candidates scanned.

Results across all 28 test boards: **1.19x aggregate speedup**, driven entirely by the hard corner-general boards. Zero capture count mismatches.

## How it works

After `buildPathEntries` strips the general tile, each `PathEntry.tiles[0]` is a neighbor of the general. Pre-grouping candidates by this value creates a `Map<moveLen, Map<neighbor, PathEntry[]>>`.

In the search loop, at each depth:
- **Zero-overlap burst**: `tiles[0]` must NOT be in `coveredMask` (otherwise the path overlaps). Skip partitions for covered neighbors.
- **Prefix-overlap burst**: `tiles[0]` MUST be in `coveredMask` (it's part of the overlap prefix). Skip partitions for uncovered neighbors.

The full mask check still runs on remaining candidates — the partition filter is necessary-but-not-sufficient. It just avoids iterating candidates that would definitely fail on their first tile.

## Why 50% on corner boards

Corner generals have degree 2 (only 2 neighbors). After burst-1 claims one neighbor, exactly one partition remains — half the candidates. This is consistent across all corner boards regardless of size.

Degree-4 generals (center/open boards) would skip 25% per covered neighbor, but these boards solve so quickly that candidate scanning isn't the bottleneck.

## Why easy boards regress slightly

On boards that solve in <100ms, total candidates checked is in the hundreds or low thousands. Skipping 94–99% of them saves microseconds, but the extra Map lookups and partition iteration overhead costs comparable microseconds. The optimization targets the wrong bottleneck on these boards.

## What this tells us

1. **Candidate scanning is a real bottleneck on hard boards.** The 1.28–1.41x speedup from a pure filtering change (no search structure changes) confirms this. The hard boards spend millions of iterations in the candidate loop.

2. **The bottleneck is board-dependent.** Easy boards solve fast because feasibility pruning eliminates most timing entries early — they never reach deep candidate scanning. Optimizing the scan doesn't help if you rarely scan.

3. **Level 2 (explicit neighbor assignment) has a clear path.** The partitioned data structure is already built. Assigning bursts to specific neighbors and searching within partitions would add earlier pruning (skip assignments where a neighbor has too few candidates) and reduce backtracking scope. The infrastructure is ready.

4. **Symmetry breaking stacks cleanly.** On degree-2 corner boards, fixing burst-1 to one specific neighbor (when both are symmetric) would halve the burst-1 search space — an independent 2x on top of this. On degree-4 symmetric boards, fixing burst-1 direction gives up to 4x.

## Implementation notes

- ~30 lines of new logic: `buildPartitionedEntries` + modified inner loop
- Required exporting ~10 private symbols from `solver-v3.ts` (additive, no behavior change)
- Candidate iteration order changes across partitions, so the specific solution found may differ from baseline — but capture counts matched on all 28 boards
