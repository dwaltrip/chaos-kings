# Burst Segmentation Analysis

Investigating question #3 from [the brainstorm doc](4.09-1-brainstorm-for-formalizing-solver-ideas.md): for each burst in a typical pattern, which region does it span (near-region vs mid-region)?

## Context

The next-gen solver hypothesis splits the board into a near-region (handled by prefix-path enumeration) and a mid-region (handled by lane decomposition + corridor model). The prefix depth D determines the boundary. This analysis asks: at a given prefix depth, how many bursts extend beyond into the mid-region?

We generated all valid timing entries for 24 captures at 50 ticks (the theoretical maximum capture count), then analyzed total path length (captures + overlap) by burst rank. Bursts are numbered B1 (longest) through B6 (shortest) following the solver's descending partition convention.

**Important caveat:** This is the full universe of timing-feasible entries — what fits within the tick/army budget at 24 captures. It does not reflect which entries actually appear in optimal solutions on real boards. Many boards can't achieve 24 captures. Board geometry constrains which overlap values are possible. The solver also tries lower capture targets (20-23). So these are upper bounds on what the framework needs to handle, not a prediction of the actual distribution. The structural observations (B1-B2 always long, B5-B6 always short) should hold since they come from the descending partition structure, but specific percentages could differ for the subset found in real solutions.

## Key findings

### The fundamental split is structural

B1-B2 always extend into the mid-region regardless of prefix depth — even at D=5, 96% and 84% extend beyond. B5-B6 are always contained in the near-region at D≥4. This isn't a parameter choice; it's inherent to the descending partition constraint and the tick budget. The two longest bursts are structurally long, the two shortest are structurally short.

### B3-B4 are the swing bursts

Whether they need mid-region lanes depends on both prefix depth and overlap budget. At D=4: B3 extends beyond in 73% of entries, B4 in 31%. These are the bursts most sensitive to the prefix depth tradeoff.

### Overlap affects shorter bursts much more than longer ones

Filtering to low-overlap entries (≤2) at D=4: B1 stays at 100%, B2 barely changes (97%), but B3 drops 73→52% and B4 drops 31→7%. This makes sense — B1 always has 0 overlap by construction, and B2's overlap is a small fraction of its total path length. For B3-B4, overlap can be a large fraction of total path length, so it significantly affects whether they spill into the mid-region.

### Practical mid-region lane count: 2-3

At D=4: always 2 lanes needed (B1, B2), usually 3 (B3 extends beyond in ~73% of entries), sometimes 4 (B4 in ~31%). Low-overlap solutions are friendlier — at overlap≤2, often just 2 lanes suffice. Since the solver tries low-overlap entries first, this is a nice alignment.

### The distribution peaks are well-separated

Each burst rank occupies a distinct distance band with limited overlap. B1 peaks at path length 7-9 (65% of entries). B2 peaks at 6-8 (76%). B3 peaks at 5-6 (56%). B4 peaks at 3-4 (62%). The diagonal structure in the counts table makes this visually clear.

## Next steps

The right prefix depth depends on the prefix-path characteristics (question #1 in the brainstorm doc). Deeper prefixes handle more bursts in the near-region but cost more to enumerate. The burst segmentation data here will be revisited once we have prefix-path data from real boards to determine where to draw the near/mid boundary.
